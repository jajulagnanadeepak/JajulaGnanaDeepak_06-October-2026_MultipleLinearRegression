MULTIPLE LINEAR REGRESSION — ADVERTISING DATASET
====================================================

Business Scenario
-----------------
A consumer goods company promotes its product using three advertising
channels:

1. Television (TV)
2. Radio
3. Newspaper

The company wants to understand how these advertising channels influence
Sales and predict future sales from planned advertising budgets.

Dataset: advertising.csv
Model: Multiple Linear Regression
Target: Sales
Features: TV, Radio, Newspaper


 IMPORT LIBRARIES
-------------------------

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.linear_model import LinearRegression
from sklearn.metrics import mean_absolute_error, mean_squared_error, r2_score


 LOAD THE DATASET
-------------------------

df = pd.read_csv("advertising.csv")

print(df.head())
print(df.shape)
print(df.columns)


 EXAMINE THE DATA
-------------------------

print(df.info())
print(df.describe())
print(df.isnull().sum())

Dataset information found:
- Rows: 200
- Columns: 4
- Columns: TV, Radio, Newspaper, Sales
- Missing values: 0

The first few rows are:

       TV  Radio  Newspaper  Sales
0   230.1   37.8       69.2   22.1
1    44.5   39.3       45.1   10.4
2    17.2   45.9       69.3   12.0
3   151.5   41.3       58.5   16.5
4   180.8   10.8       58.4   17.9


 SELECT FEATURES AND TARGET
------------------------------------

X = df[["TV", "Radio", "Newspaper"]]
y = df["Sales"]

X contains the advertising budgets.
y contains the actual Sales values.


 SPLIT THE DATA
-----------------------

X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(
    X, y,
    test_size=0.20,
    random_state=42
)

Result:
- Training data: 160 rows
- Testing data: 40 rows

The model learns from the 160 training records and is evaluated on
40 unseen records.


 CREATE AND TRAIN THE MODEL
-----------------------------------

model = LinearRegression()

model.fit(X_train, y_train)

The model learns the relationship:

Sales = Intercept
      + TV coefficient × TV
      + Radio coefficient × Radio
      + Newspaper coefficient × Newspaper


 PREDICT SALES FOR UNSEEN DATA
--------------------------------------

y_pred = model.predict(X_test)

The model now predicts Sales for the 40 test records that it did not
use during training.


 PREDICT SALES FOR THE GIVEN BUDGET
-------------------------------------------

Budget:
TV = 150
Radio = 20
Newspaper = 30

new_budget = pd.DataFrame({
    "TV": [150],
    "Radio": [20],
    "Newspaper": [30]
})

predicted_sales = model.predict(new_budget)

print(predicted_sales)

RESULT:
Predicted Sales ≈ 15.04

Therefore, when the company spends:
TV = 150
Radio = 20
Newspaper = 30

the model predicts approximately 15.04 units of Sales.


 EVALUATE THE MODEL
---------------------------

mae = mean_absolute_error(y_test, y_pred)
mse = mean_squared_error(y_test, y_pred)
rmse = np.sqrt(mse)
r2 = r2_score(y_test, y_pred)

print("MAE:", mae)
print("MSE:", mse)
print("RMSE:", rmse)
print("R2 Score:", r2)

RESULTS WITH random_state=42:

MAE  = 1.2748
MSE  = 2.9078
RMSE = 1.7052
R²   = 0.9059

Interpretation:
- MAE of about 1.27 means the prediction is off by about 1.27 Sales
  units on average.
- RMSE of about 1.71 gives a slightly stronger penalty to larger errors.
- R² of about 0.906 means the model explains approximately 90.6% of
  the variation in Sales on this test split.

 INTERPRET THE MODEL COEFFICIENTS
------------------------------------------

print("Intercept:", model.intercept_)
print("Coefficients:", model.coef_)

RESULTS:

Intercept  = 4.7141

TV coefficient        = 0.0545
Radio coefficient     = 0.1009
Newspaper coefficient = 0.0043

Approximate model equation for this train/test split:

Sales = 4.7141
      + 0.0545 × TV
      + 0.1009 × Radio
      + 0.0043 × Newspaper


Meaning:

1. TV
   Keeping Radio and Newspaper constant, increasing TV advertising
   by 1 unit is associated with an increase of about 0.0545 Sales units.

2. Radio
   Keeping TV and Newspaper constant, increasing Radio advertising
   by 1 unit is associated with an increase of about 0.1009 Sales units.

3. Newspaper
   Keeping TV and Radio constant, increasing Newspaper advertising
   by 1 unit is associated with an increase of about 0.0043 Sales units.


WHICH MEDIUM HAS THE STRONGEST IMPACT?
---------------------------------------

Based directly on the fitted raw coefficients in this model:

Radio has the largest coefficient:
0.1009

Therefore, Radio has the strongest estimated effect per one-unit
increase in advertising spend in this particular fitted model.

Newspaper has the smallest coefficient:
0.0043

Therefore, Newspaper has the least estimated effect per one-unit
increase.

IMPORTANT:
Raw coefficients should be compared carefully because the three
features have different distributions/scales. If we standardize all
features before comparing coefficients, TV shows the strongest
relative contribution, followed by Radio, while Newspaper is almost
negligible.

For this business question, the safest conclusion is:
- Radio has the strongest per-unit coefficient in the fitted model.
- TV is also a very important predictor and has the strongest
  standardized contribution.
- Newspaper contributes very little compared with TV and Radio.

 TECHNICAL IMPROVEMENT
--------------------------------

Technical improvement:

Use cross-validation and feature engineering.

For example:
- Use K-Fold Cross-Validation instead of relying on one train/test split.

  Gradient Boosting.
- Tune and compare models using MAE, RMSE and R².
